Overall SparkEV Workflow & Architecture

SparkEV is built entirely on real-world telemetry and comprises four interconnected modules feeding a unified recommendation engine:

The SparkEV Pipeline Architecture (Figure 1 in Paper)

M1: Battery Stress TUM 70 trips → XGBoost [FL]
M2: Charging Faults Autosun (2,649) → XGBoost
M3: SOH & Risk Zenodo SOH → Direct AR
M4: Energy / SOC TUM trips → Elastic Net [FL]

All four module outputs are individually explained with TreeSHAP feature attributions, fused into a single risk profile by the recommendation engine, and displayed on the driver dashboard. Modules M1 and M4 are additionally trained via Federated Learning (FedAvg / FedPer).

Module 1: Driving Behavior to Battery Stress

Lead: Rithanya K. A. (CB.SC.U4CSE23140)

Battery stress is not measured directly. A Battery Stress Index (BSI) on a 0–100 scale is computed for every 60 s driving window (30 s step) from four min–max normalized components:

$$\text{BSI} = 100 \left( w_T S_T + w_C S_C + w_D S_D + w_S S_S \right) \quad \text{--- Eq. (1)}$$

where \(S_T, S_C, S_D, S_S\) derive from peak temperature, peak C-rate, SoC drop, and SoC excursion outside the 20–80% band (weights: \(w_T=0.35, w_C=0.30, w_D=0.20, w_S=0.15\)).

Physics-Informed Semi-Empirical Refinement

Because literature weights are assumptions, the thermal and C-rate terms are replaced by a semi-empirical capacity-fade model:

$$Q_{\text{loss}} = B \exp\left( -\frac{E_a}{R T} \right) C^m (Ah)^z \quad \text{--- Eq. (2)}$$

Fitted by log-linear regression on 842 measured aging cycles from NASA and CALCE cells, explaining 88.6% of calibration variance (\(m = 0.80, p = 0.04; z = 0.54, p < 0.001; E_a = 27.8\text{ kJ/mol}\)). Evaluated on each window's peak temperature, peak C-rate, and delivered charge, Eq. (2) replaces \(w_T S_T + w_C S_C\).

Key SHAP Discovery: This refinement raises XGBoost CV \(R^2\) from 0.708 ± 0.094 to 0.831 ± 0.040 (held-out \(R^2 = 0.805\), MAE 3.23). Crucially, ambient temperature drops from rank 1 to rank 5, while driver-controllable factors—maximum speed, average speed, and mean acceleration—become the top 3 drivers!

Module 2: Charging-Session Anomaly Detection

Lead: Irene Divya J. (CB.SC.U4CSE23167)

Raw telemetry is aggregated into 2,649 sessions (57 features from SOC, voltage, current, power, temperature, energy). An XGBoost classifier is weighted by the negative-to-positive ratio (8.765) to handle the 10.2% class imbalance:

  • XGBoost achieves 99.62% Accuracy and F1 = 0.981 (ROC-AUC 0.999): Detects 52 of 54 faulty test sessions without a single false alarm.
  • Baselines Fail: TCN detects 0 faults (F1 0.000); LSTM detects only 1 of 54 (F1 0.036); Isolation Forest misses 23 faults (F1 0.713).
  • Feature Resilience: Removing the top 5 energy features leaves performance identical at 99.62% and F1 = 0.981, proving fault signatures are robustly distributed across voltage, SOC, power, and temperature.

Module 3: Battery Health and Degradation Risk

Lead: Shravanthi S. (CB.SC.U4CSE23147)

Forecasts SOH ten cycles ahead from features available at cycle \(t\) (lags, drops, degradation rates, rolling trends). The Direct Autoregressive (AR) model achieves the highest accuracy:

$$\widehat{\text{SOH}}(t + 10) = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} \beta_i \text{SOH}(t - i)$$

Direct AR achieves \(R^2 = 0.9591\) with MAE = 0.00657 on the chronological test set (18,065 targets from Zenodo), avoiding recursive error accumulation.

Causal Accelerated-Degradation Detector: SOH history is smoothed with a causal rolling median, comparing recent rate \(r_{\text{recent}}\) (5 observations) with long-term rate \(r_{\text{long}}\) (20 observations): \(\Delta_{\text{acc}} = r_{\text{recent}} - r_{\text{long}}\). Confirmed if persisting in ≥2 of the last 3 observations, providing early warnings before significant capacity fade occurs.

Module 4: Energy Consumption & In-Trip Arrival SOC

Lead: Saravana Priyaa C. R. (CB.SC.U4CSE23149)

Two Elastic Net models are deployed on the TUM Munich real-driving dataset:

  • Stable-23 Energy Model: Selected 23 features consistent across all 50 cross-validation folds; achieves \(R^2 = 0.897\) (MAE 0.0077 kWh/km), beating full 77-feature Elastic Net (0.854), Ridge (0.819), and tree ensembles (0.72–0.74). Top SHAP drivers: battery current, cabin temperature, heating power, high-speed share.
  • In-Trip Arrival SOC Model: Evaluated on 85 leakage-safe features strictly before the trip cutoff:
    • At 25% trip: \(R^2 = 0.932\) (MAE 2.54 pp)
    • At 50% trip: \(R^2 = 0.976\) (MAE 1.48 pp)
    • At 75% trip: \(R^2 = 0.984\) (MAE 1.22 pp)

Personalized Federated Learning (FedPer)

Lead: Gayathri U. (CB.SC.U4CSE23026)

FedPer splits the model into shared base parameters \(\theta_B\) and a client-specific head \(\theta_{P,k}\), aggregating only the base to preserve driver privacy:

$$\theta_B^{(r+1)} = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{n_k}{n} \theta_{B,k}^{(r)}, \quad \theta_{P,k}^{(r+1)} \text{ stays on client } k \quad \text{--- Eq. (3)}$$

Tested on 51 client trips from TUM. FedAvg nearly matches centralized arrival-SOC (\(R^2 = 0.973\) vs \(0.976\); \(\Delta R^2 \le 0.03\)). For battery stress, FedPer lowers the local error for 32 of 51 established clients (median MAE 2.81 vs 3.51 for FedAvg). Early stopping at round 30 prevents cold-start overfitting.

On-Device Edge Deployment on ESP32 (Table 7)

Lead: Saravana Priyaa C. R. (CB.SC.U4CSE23149)

All three XGBoost models of Modules 1–3 were converted into C header files and run on an ESP32 microcontroller at 240 MHz by a lightweight tree-traversal routine:

Model Sample On-Device Output Inference Time (ms)
M1: Battery Stress BSI 62.39, "MODERATE" 3.35 ms
M2: Charging Anomaly P(faulty) = 1.000, "Faulty" 1.92 ms
M3: SOH / Degradation Risk SOH 91.76%, "LOW" 3.38 ms
Total (Sequential) SH1106 OLED Display + JSON over Wi-Fi 8.65 ms

Over 20 transmissions, server processing took \(17.5 \pm 2.0\text{ ms}\), while round-trip time over mobile hotspot averaged 188 ms. The entire cycle finishes in under 1 second!
