Overall SparkEV Workflow & Architecture

Electric vehicle management has historically operated in isolation: battery monitoring merely reports state-of-charge, eco-driving guidance focuses on energy conservation without considering cell fatigue, and charging locators overlook battery longevity. SparkEV unites these streams into an integrated intelligence framework built entirely on real-world telemetry.

The Interconnected SparkEV Workflow (Figure 1 in Paper)

Real-world telemetry feeds four predictive modules: driving telemetry drives Module 1 (Battery Stress), charging session records feed Module 2 (Charging Anomaly Detection), battery cycling records train Module 3 (SOH Forecasting and Risk Scoring), and trip telemetry powers Module 4 (Energy Consumption and Arrival SOC). Every prediction is interpreted through TreeSHAP explainability, and a unified recommendation engine converts the outputs into actionable in-car alerts, personalized tips, and interactive what-if comparisons.

M1: Battery Stress TUM 70 trips → XGBoost [Federated]
M2: Charging Faults Autosun (2,649) → Weighted XGBoost
M3: SOH & Risk Zenodo SOH → Direct Autoregression
M4: Energy / SOC TUM trips → Stable-23 Elastic Net

Module 1: Driving Behavior to Battery Stress

Working Principle & Approach

Because battery stress cannot be directly measured by an in-car sensor, Module 1 constructs a physics-informed Battery Stress Index (BSI) over continuous 60-second driving windows. Instead of using arbitrary heuristic weights, the model replaces thermal and electrical current factors with a semi-empirical capacity-fade model fitted to measured cell degradation data from NASA and CALCE. An XGBoost regressor then learns to predict this stress index directly from observable driving behaviors—such as vehicle speed and acceleration—without using internal battery measurements that could cause data leakage.

Module Inputs
  • Speed statistics: instantaneous, mean, and peak velocities
  • Acceleration dynamics: acceleration rates and jerk statistics
  • Driving events: counts of hard accelerations and emergency braking
  • Operational factors: idle duration and regenerative braking share
  • Trip context: driver aggressiveness score, ambient temperature, and route categories
Module Outputs
  • Battery Stress Index (BSI): normalized score on a 0–100 scale
  • Risk Tiers: classified into Low (<40), Moderate (40–70), or High (≥70)
  • Explainable SHAP Contributions: quantitative impact of peak speed and acceleration
  • Driver Recommendations: actionable advice prioritizing speed reduction and smoother pedal work

Mathematical Formulation

The semi-empirical capacity-fade model is formulated as:

$$Q_{\text{loss}} = B \exp\left( -\frac{E_a}{R T} \right) C^m (Ah)^z \quad \text{--- Eq. (2)}$$

Fitted by log-linear regression on 842 measured aging cycles, explaining 88.6% of calibration variance (\(m = 0.80, z = 0.54, E_a = 27.8\text{ kJ/mol}\)). This formulation raises prediction accuracy to \(R^2 = 0.831\) and shifts the top SHAP drivers to maximum speed, average speed, and mean acceleration.

Module 2: Charging-Session Anomaly Detection

Working Principle & Approach

Evaluates entire electric vehicle charging sessions to identify faulty hardware, abnormal power cutoffs, and thermal issues from the driver's perspective. Using 2,649 real sessions from the Autosun/PolyU dataset, a weighted XGBoost classifier is trained to account for the natural 10.2% fault class imbalance, detecting damaged or malfunctioning chargers without generating false alarms.

Module Inputs
  • Voltage characteristics: initial, maximum, and average pack voltage
  • Current measurements: current ripple, maximum charging current, and amperage taper
  • Energy and power: total kilowatt-hours delivered and charging power profiles
  • Battery state & thermal: starting and ending SOC, and cell temperature rise
Module Outputs
  • Session Classification: binary label indicating Normal or Faulty session
  • Fault Probability: probabilistic confidence score for the detected anomaly
  • Root-Cause Diagnostic Flags: identifies whether fault is driven by voltage, power, or thermal instability

Module 3: Battery Health and Degradation Risk

Working Principle & Approach

Forecasts battery State-of-Health (SOH) ten full cycles ahead using a Direct Autoregressive (AR) approach, which predicts the multi-step horizon directly in a single step to avoid error accumulation. In addition, an accelerated-degradation detector compares the battery's recent capacity fade rate against its long-term trend, flagging instances where degradation suddenly speeds up.

Module Inputs
  • Historical SOH lags: previous capacity percentages from earlier cycles
  • Degradation rates: short-term (5-cycle) and long-term (20-cycle) causal rolling slopes
  • Cycle indicators: total operating cycles and cumulative ampere-hour throughput
Module Outputs
  • Ten-Cycle SOH Forecast: predicted capacity percentage (\(R^2 = 0.9591\))
  • Health Category: Low (≥85%), Moderate (80–85%), High (70–80%), or Critical (<70%)
  • Accelerated Wear Warning: 0–100 risk score detecting abnormal degradation speed

Module 4: Energy Consumption & In-Trip Arrival SOC

Working Principle & Approach

Employs two Elastic Net regression models trained on real driving trips around Munich. The first model predicts the vehicle's overall energy rate from 23 highly stable features, while the second model performs in-trip estimation of final arrival state-of-charge based strictly on telemetry observed during the first 25%, 50%, or 75% of the trip.

Module Inputs
  • Electrical loads: battery current and traction motor power demand
  • Auxiliary systems: cabin heating power and air-conditioning usage
  • Kinematics: high-speed motorway driving share and average cruising velocity
  • Early-trip telemetry: 85 leakage-safe features computed before trip cutoff
Module Outputs
  • Trip Energy Rate: predicted electricity usage in kWh per kilometer (\(R^2 = 0.897\))
  • Arrival SOC: predicted remaining battery percentage upon reaching destination (\(R^2 = 0.976\))
  • Range Confidence: safety buffer indicating whether charging is required en route

Personalized Federated Learning (FedPer)

Preserves user data privacy by partitioning the neural network into two parts: shared base representation layers trained collaboratively across the electric vehicle fleet, and a local personalization head trained exclusively on each vehicle. Only model weight deltas are shared with the central coordinator; raw telemetry and GPS traces never leave the car.

$$\theta_B^{(r+1)} = \sum_{k=1}^K \frac{n_k}{n} \theta_{B,k}^{(r)}, \quad \theta_{P,k}^{(r+1)} \text{ stays on client } k \quad \text{--- Eq. (3)}$$

Evaluated on 51 client trips, FedPer lowers the local prediction error for 32 of 51 established vehicle clients compared to standard federated averaging, while federated arrival SOC estimates closely match centralized training accuracy.
